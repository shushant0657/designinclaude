Make your app's rulebook and teach it to Claude

<you-will-learn>

  • The 7 things every app rulebook has
  • How to make yours from screenshots, step by step
  • How to put it inside Claude and test it

In Lesson 1 we learned the big idea: write your app's rules down once, and Claude follows them forever. Today you make that rulebook. Grab the screenshots from your homework.

Two ways to do this lesson: do every step yourself by chatting with Claude (free, about 45 minutes), or use the Brand-to-Skill skill from the pack, which does all the steps for you in about 15 minutes. Both give the same result.

What goes in a rulebook?

Think of it like the instructions for a LEGO set. Here are the seven parts:

PartWhat it meansEveryday example
ColoursWhich colour is used for which job“Main buttons are dark green. Background is cream.”
Text sizesHow big titles, normal words and small notes are“Titles are big and bold. Notes are small and grey.”
SpacingHow much empty room goes around things“Always leave a finger's width between cards.”
PiecesWhat buttons, cards and lists look like“Buttons are rounded and full width.”
VoiceHow the app talks“Friendly and short. Say ‘Add money’, not ‘Initiate transfer’.”
Never listThings your app never does“Never use purple. Never use fake text.”
Self-checkA checklist Claude runs before showing you a design“Did I only use our colours? Is the main button easy to find?”

Step 1 — Give every colour a job

Don't just say “green”. Say what green is for. Upload your screenshots to Claude and type:

Look at these screenshots of my app. List every colour you see, and for each one tell me its job: background, cards, main text, light text, main buttons, borders, success, warning or error.
New word: tokenA colour or size with a name that says its job, like button-colour instead of “green”. Names help Claude pick the right one.

Step 2 — Measure the breathing room

Most AI designs feel “almost right” because the spacing is off, not the colours. Ask:

From the screenshots, how much space is at the edge of the screen, inside cards, between list items and between sections? Give me a simple spacing scale.

Step 3 — Describe your 5 most-used pieces

Usually: a button, a text box, a card, a list row, and one piece special to your app (like a price tag or a progress bar). Ask Claude to describe each one, including how it looks when pressed, turned off or loading.

New word: stateThe different ways one thing can look. A button can be normal, pressed, disabled (greyed out) or loading (spinning).

Step 4 — Write your Never list

This is the secret weapon against boring AI looks. Start with these and add your own:

  • Never use colours that aren't in our list
  • Never add gradients or shadows unless our app has them
  • Never write fake text like “Lorem ipsum”
  • Never put a box inside another box

Step 5 — Look at it all on one page

Ask Claude: Make one page that shows all my colours, text sizes, spacing and pieces. This is called a specimen page. If anything looks wrong, fix the rule, not just the page.

Step 6 — Save it as a skill and test it

Ask Claude: Turn everything we decided into a design-system skill I can install. Claude gives you a folder. Then install it:

  • Claude website or app: zip the folder, then go to Settings → Skills → Upload.
  • Claude Code (for coders): put the folder in ~/.claude/skills/.

Now the fun part. Open a new chat and type one line: Design the settings screen. It should look like your app. If something still looks generic, ask: “What rule would have stopped this? Add it to my design system.”

Menu names can change as Claude updates. If you can't find Skills, search “skills” in Claude's help centre.

With the pack: just type Set up my design system for <my app> and drop in your screenshots. Brand-to-Skill asks you a few easy questions, builds the rulebook and the specimen page, and tests it for you.

You did it. You're now Level 10: Claude knows your app. Next, we make real screens.
