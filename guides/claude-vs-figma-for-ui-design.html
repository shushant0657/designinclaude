Claude vs Figma for UI design

Short answer: they're good at different things, and most teams that design in Claude still keep Figma for some work. Here's how a product design manager who now does most design work in Claude splits it.

ClaudeFigma
Exploring directionsVery fast — 4 directions in one messageManual, slower
Every state of a screenAsked for in one line, clickableDrawn frame by frame
Real content & edge casesLong names, ₹0, 20 items — instantlyManual
Pixel-level craftGood with a strong design systemBest
Live collaboration on canvasReview pages with commentsBest
Developer handoffSpecs + acceptance checks written for youInspect panel, Dev Mode
Staying on brandOnly with a design-system skillLibraries & components

Use Claude when…

  • you're exploring or prototyping and want to try many directions fast;
  • you need every state and edge case of a flow;
  • you want a review, a flow check or a handoff spec written for you.

Use Figma when…

  • you're crafting final visual details or illustrations;
  • a big team is editing the same canvas live;
  • your organisation's library lives there.

The catch with Claude

Out of the box, Claude doesn't know your brand, so everything looks generic. The fix is to put your design system into Claude as a skill — then its screens look like your product.

Questions

Can Claude replace Figma for UI design?

For exploring, prototyping, states, reviews and handoff, often yes. For pixel-perfect final craft and live multi-person canvas editing, Figma is still stronger. Many teams use both.

Is designing in Claude faster than Figma?

For exploration, states and edge cases, usually much faster, because you describe what you want in a sentence and get a clickable result.

Can I move Claude designs into Figma?

Yes. Claude's designs are HTML, which several tools can import into Figma as editable layers.
