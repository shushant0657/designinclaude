How to design UI with Claude, step by step

Claude can design real, clickable app screens in a chat. The catch: ask it cold and you'll get the same thing everyone gets — a purple gradient, a big number, three icons and “Lorem ipsum”. This guide shows the workflow that fixes that, in seven steps.

1. Understand why AI designs look the same

Claude writes by predicting what usually comes next. If it knows nothing about your product, “usual” means the average of every app it has seen. Your job is to change what “usual” means — by giving it your rules.

2. Teach Claude your design system (once)

Show Claude 4–6 screenshots of your app and ask it to describe the colours, text, spacing and pieces. Then give every colour a job (“main button”, not “blue”), set a type scale and spacing scale, and add a Never list (“no purple gradients, no lorem ipsum, no card inside a card”). Save it all as a design-system skill so every new chat starts on brand. No design system yet? Start from 4–6 apps you love.

I want to build a design system for my app from these screenshots, step by step. Don't build anything yet. First, describe what you see: the overall feel, colours, text, spacing and the main pieces.

3. Ask for a screen in three lines

Answer three questions: who is on the screen, where they came from, and what must be there. Ask for a five-line plan before Claude builds.

Design the Add money screen. A member is looking at their savings goals and wants to add money to one goal. It must show the goal, the amount, quick buttons and a pay button.

4. Ask for every version of the screen

Pros design five versions of every screen: normal, loading, error, success and empty. Ask Claude for all of them in one artifact with tabs to switch between them.

5. Steer with one word, polish in passes

Not sure what's wrong? Try one word: bolder, quieter, distill, clarify, denser. Then polish one thing at a time — type, then spacing, then motion — so nothing else breaks.

6. Review it like a senior designer

Ask Claude to review against your design system in a fixed order (main job, information, spacing, text, colour, consistency, versions, accessibility) and sort issues into P1, P2 and P3. Fix P1 and P2, then stop. Ask for problems, not praise — models can lean towards telling you what you want to hear.

7. Hand it to engineers

A picture isn't a spec. Ask Claude for a developer handoff: every element with its component and tokens, what happens on tap, edge cases, and simple checks anyone can test (“Tapping ₹500 adds ₹500 to the amount”).

Questions

Is Claude good at UI design?

Claude is strong at layout, states, copy and turning rules into consistent screens. Its weak spot is your brand — it doesn't know it until you teach it. With a design-system skill, its screens look like your product.

Can Claude replace Figma?

For a lot of work — exploring, prototyping, states, reviews and handoff — yes. Many teams design in Claude and move only final, shared assets to Figma.

Do I need to code to design in Claude?

No. You talk in plain language. Claude makes the HTML previews; you never have to touch the code.
