39 Claude prompts for UI design

These are the exact prompts used in the Design in Claude course, in the order you'd use them — from your very first chat to handing a screen to engineers. Tap any prompt to copy it. Replace anything in [brackets] with your own details.

The one rule that makes every prompt better: put your design system into Claude first (Level 10). Without it, even a great prompt gives you the same generic purple app everyone gets.

Level 0 · What is Claude, what is design, and why AI designs all look the same

Explain what an app designer does, like I'm 10 years old.
Make a simple to-do list app screen I can click. It should have a title, three tasks with checkboxes, and an Add button.
Make the title bigger, put the Add button at the bottom, and make it dark green.
Design a home screen for a savings app.

Level 10 · Make your app's rulebook and teach it to Claude

I don't have a design system yet. My product is [one line] for [who]. It should feel [3 words]. Here are apps I love and what I like in each: [list]. Make a remix table: one row per design decision, which reference it comes from, and the value you propose. Take ideas, not identities — the result must look like a new product, not a copy.
I want to build a design system for my app from these screenshots, step by step. Don't build anything yet. First, describe what you see: the overall feel, colours, text, spacing and the main pieces (buttons, cards, lists). Then tell me what you can't tell from the screenshots.
Before the visuals: my app is used by [who], usually [where/how]. Their three main jobs are [jobs]. Accessibility needs: [needs]. The design must earn [trust / speed / delight]. Add this as a “Product” section at the top of my design system.
List every colour you see in the screenshots with its hex code. Then give each one a job from this list: page background, card background, subtle background, main text, light text, text on buttons, main button, main button pressed, accent, light accent, border, success, warning, error. If a job has no colour, suggest one that fits.
Also add an “Ask first” list to my design system: things we haven't decided yet (like dark mode). Never guess these — ask me.
Now text. What font does the app use (or the closest free one)? Make a type scale: display, h1, h2, body, small and label — each with size, line height and weight. Base it on what you see in the screenshots.
Measure the spacing in the screenshots: the gap at the screen edge, inside cards, between items in a list, and between sections. Turn it into a spacing scale on a 4px grid (4, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24, 32, 48). Also tell me the corner roundness of buttons, cards and sheets, and whether the app uses shadows or borders.
Write a recipe for each of these components: button, text input, card, list row, and [your special piece]. For each: what it's made of, its variants (like primary and secondary), every state (default, pressed, focused, disabled, loading, error), sizes, and which tokens it uses. Keep each recipe short.
From the words in the screenshots, write voice rules: tone in 3 words, how buttons are written (verb first?), how errors are written, how money, dates and numbers are formatted, and 10 words to avoid.
Add a Never list to the design system. Include these: [paste the list]. Then add 5 more based on what would make a screen look generic compared to my app.
Make a specimen page as an artifact: every colour with its name and hex, every text size, the spacing scale, and the five components in all their states.
Turn everything we decided into a design-system skill folder: a SKILL.md with the rules, the Never list and a 10-point self-check, a tokens.css file, and a DESIGN.md that describes the same system in plain markdown. The skill should load whenever I ask to design a screen for [app name]. Give me the folder as a zip.
Design the settings screen for [app name].

Level 25 · Make your first real screens

Design the Add money screen. A member is looking at their savings goals and wants to add money to one goal. It must show the goal, the amount, quick buttons like ₹500 and ₹1,000, and how they'll pay. After paying they go back to their goals.
Before designing, give me a 5-line plan: the one main action, what goes at the top, the order of content, which components you'll use, and which versions (states) this screen needs.
Build the screen with all relevant versions in one artifact, in a phone-sized frame, with buttons at the top to switch between versions.
Now show me the edge cases: a very long goal name, an amount of ₹0, someone with 1 goal and someone with 20 goals.
Show me 4 different directions for this screen side by side: one safe, one bold, two in between. Vary the layout and what's most important — never the colours, fonts or components from my design system. Name each one and tell me its trade-off.
We're designing the Add money feature. Screens: Goals list → Add money sheet → Pay with UPI → Success. Build them one at a time; use the same components and wording across all of them. Start with the Goals list.

Level 50 · Learn to spot what's wrong with a design

Review this screen against my design system using these 8 checks in order: main job, information, layout and spacing, text, colour and contrast, consistency, versions, accessibility. For each problem give: where, what's wrong, why it matters, the exact fix (token, value, component or words), and a priority P1/P2/P3. Sort by priority.
Apply fixes 1, 2, 4 and 5. Skip 3 — we keep that on purpose. Show me before and after side by side.
Turn this review into feedback for a junior designer: start with what works, then the P1s and P2s with the reason for each, in a supportive tone. Keep it under 150 words.

Level 75 · Check the whole journey and get feedback

Here are the screens of my Add money feature. Write the flow as a line with arrows, from where the person starts to where they end. Mark every place where they could go back, leave, or hit a problem.
Make a table: one row for each recurring thing (top bar, back button, main button position and wording, title style, money format, date format, words for the same concept), and one column for each screen. Highlight anything that doesn't match.
List the screens and states missing from this flow. Check: form errors, payment failed, payment pending or unsure, no internet, session expired, leaving without saving, success with next steps, and edge cases like out of stock, expired or already done.
Make a review page for these screens: screen list on the left, version tabs on top, the screen in the middle in a phone frame, and a comment panel on the right where each comment is tagged Question, Suggestion or Must fix. Add a “Copy all feedback” button. At the top: goal, what feedback I want, what to ignore, deadline.
Here's the feedback from the review. Group it by screen. Apply every Must fix. For Suggestions, tell me which ones you'd take and why. List the Questions I need to answer.
Play my product manager in a design review. She cares about conversion and shipping on time, and she's sceptical of new patterns. I'll present the Add money flow. Push back like she really would. After each round, step out of character and tell me what worked and what didn't.

Level 90 · Hand your design to engineers (and share it with your team)

Write a developer handoff for this screen using my design system. Include: an element table (element, component and variant, tokens, content source, behaviour), every state with what triggers it, interactions, content rules (max lengths, truncation, formats), edge cases, acceptance criteria as testable sentences, and open questions with an owner.
Here are my answers to the open questions: [answers]. Update the spec and remove the questions you've resolved.
Here's my design and a screenshot of the built screen. List every difference: spacing, colours, text, missing states, wrong behaviour. Sort by P1/P2/P3.

Level 100 · Level 100: build your own design tools

I'm a designer. Here's what my week looks like: [describe it]. Suggest 5 small tools I could build with you that would save me or my team time. For each: the problem, who uses it, and the smallest version that would help.
Build a simple web page that shows my team's projects by month. Columns: designer, project, status, link, last update. Add a month switcher at the top. Use my design system. Start with this data: [paste a few rows].
Turn this into a skill: every time I finish a prototype, check that every screen has all its versions, test it at phone, tablet and desktop sizes, and report anything broken as a list. Name it “prototype-qa”.
I built [tool]. Help me turn it into a small product: who it's for, a one-line pitch, a price, a landing page in my design system, and a launch post.

Questions

What is the best prompt to design UI with Claude?

There isn't one magic prompt. The biggest jump in quality comes from giving Claude your design system first (colours with jobs, type scale, spacing, components and a Never list), then asking in three lines: who is on the screen, where they came from, and what must be there.

Can Claude design UI screens?

Yes. Claude builds working screens as artifacts (live HTML previews) you can click, review on phone sizes and hand to developers. With a design-system skill it follows your brand instead of a generic style.

Do these prompts work in the free Claude plan?

Most do. Saving your design system as a reusable skill needs a plan that supports Skills; on other plans you can paste the rules into a Project instead.
