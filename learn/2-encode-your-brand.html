Encode your brand into Claude

<you-will-learn>

  • What a design-system skill contains
  • How to extract it from what you already have
  • How to check it and install it

In this lesson you'll create the single most useful file in your AI workflow: a design-system skill for your product. You can write it by hand following the structure below, or let the Brand-to-Skill skill in the pack do the extraction for you in about 15 minutes.

What goes into a design-system skill

SectionWhat it holdsWhy it matters
TokensColours by role (action, surface, muted text…), type scale, spacing scale, radii, borders/shadowsClaude uses names, not raw hex — so nothing off-brand sneaks in
Principles3 adjectives and what they mean in decisionsBreaks ties the way your team would
Component recipesAnatomy, variants, every state, sizes, a tiny code snippetThe biggest difference between "close" and "ours"
PatternsEmpty, loading, error, forms, money, datesCovers the screens people forget
VoiceButton verbs, error tone, words to avoidCopy is half of what makes UI feel like a product
Do / NeverYour rules plus anti-generic rules"Never" rules stop the stock-AI look
Self-checkA 10-point list Claude runs before showing a screenCatches mistakes before you see them

Step 1 — Name colours by role, not by hue

"Green" tells Claude nothing about when to use it. --color-action-primary does. Map every colour to a job: page background, surface, subtle surface, text, muted text, primary action, accent, border, success, warning, error. If a colour has no job, it probably shouldn't be in product UI.

Step 2 — Capture the rhythm

Most "almost right" AI screens fail on spacing, not colour. Write down your screen padding, card padding, gap between list items and gap between sections. Snap them to your spacing scale.

Step 3 — Write recipes for the 5 components you use most

Usually: button, input, card, list row, and one product-specific component (a price tag, a goal card, a seat). For each, list the states — default, hover, pressed, focus, disabled, loading, error — and what changes in each.

Step 4 — Add your Never list

Start with the universal ones: no colours outside the tokens, no gradients or shadows unless the brand uses them, no centred page titles in product UI, no lorem ipsum, no cards inside cards. Then add yours — the things that make your team wince.

Step 5 — Check it visually

Render a specimen page: every colour, type size, spacing step and core component on one page. If something doesn't look like your product, fix the skill, not just the page.

Step 6 — Test with one real screen

New chat, one line: "Design the <screen from your backlog>." If it still looks generic, your skill is missing a specific — usually a component recipe or a Never rule. Ask Claude: "What rule would have prevented this? Add it to the design system."

With the pack: say Set up my design system for <product> and share your screenshots or tokens. Brand-to-Skill runs all six steps, asks you to confirm the gaps in one reply, builds the skill, tokens file and specimen page, and tests it on a real screen.

Install it

  • Claude app: Settings → Skills → Upload skill (zip the folder first).
  • Claude Code: put the folder in ~/.claude/skills/, or .claude/skills/ in your repo to share with your team.

Menu names can change as Claude updates; search "skills" in Claude's help centre if you can't find it.
